# GermanyCW Infrastructure Candidates

This document records the initial infrastructure inventory and the boundary
between external geographic meaning and DCS runtime state. It is an analysis
document, not a list of automatically authorized strategic targets.

## Source inventory

The current GermanyCW viewport cache contains 4,732,330 topography features
from 29 non-empty Geofabrik shards. A deduplicated scan of the existing
`topography_infrastructure` layer found 485,141 raw candidates:

- 336,444 bridges, now superseded by the typed transport-infrastructure cache
- 56,359 industrial areas
- 33,647 individual storage tanks
- 21,753 individual silos
- 14,142 works
- 4,418 power plants
- 3,971 water works
- 3,267 wastewater plants
- 1,420 pipeline-related objects
- 751 gas facilities
- 713 factories
- 649 depots
- 495 harbours
- 456 oil facilities
- 162 ports
- 59 shipyards

These values describe modern OSM source features, not operational sites. They
also show why raw features cannot become objectives directly. One refinery or
port can contain many tanks, buildings, piers, conveyors, and overlapping
industrial polygons.

The Mecklenburg-Vorpommern shard contains 8,104 raw infrastructure features.
After excluding 5,140 bridges, its largest groups are 1,008 industrial areas,
745 tanks, 530 silos, 161 works, 96 power plants, 70 water works, 68 wastewater
plants, and 18 harbours. Only 1,525 of all 8,104 features have a name.

## DCS representation boundary

DCS Cold War Germany explicitly models road and rail networks, power plants and
the electricity grid, industrial sites, mines, tunnels, stadiums, rail yards
and stations, military sites, ports, and selected landmarks. That means these
categories are useful candidates for comparison, but does not mean every OSM
site exists in the current DCS map phase or matches its modern shape.

The runtime distinguishes three relevant representations:

1. `AIRBASE`, `STATIC`, `UNIT`, and mission zones have explicit mission-level
   identity and are straightforward to mirror.
2. Terrain buildings, bridges, and similar map objects can be returned as DCS
   `SceneryObject` instances by a bounded `world.searchObjects` request. They
   expose position, type/description, existence, and life and can participate
   in Hit and Death events.
3. Some visible terrain detail may not provide a useful independently
   addressable object. It can still be geographic context but cannot be given a
   reliable DCS damage state.

Scenery discovery supplies runtime objects, not site semantics. Python must
therefore own the association between a normalized site such as a power plant
and the nearby DCS scenery objects that represent its components. The
association should be generated by an explicit bounded verification pass and
stored in the theater cache. It must not require a periodic full-map scenery
scan.

## Implemented foundation

The SDK now provides a shared `InfrastructureSite` base with distinct
`EnergySite` and `MilitarySite` types. Candidate admission is controlled by an
`InfrastructureCandidatePolicy` selected per theater. The GermanyCW policy
uses 1989 as its scenario reference year and excludes wind, solar, biogas, and
battery sites. Generic policies exclude nothing, so maps which intentionally
represent these technologies can retain them.

Build the compact energy-site artifact from the indexed topography cache with:

```powershell
python tools/build_infrastructure_sites.py
```

The current source produces 2,499 admitted energy sites from 4,418 unique OSM
power-plant objects. Use `--include-modern-energy` only for a scenario that
deliberately wants the modern sources. The output is
`tmp/topography/GermanyCW-infrastructure-sites.geojson`.

The bridge also exposes `await bridge.survey_scenery(latitude, longitude)` for
local verification. Its defaults are a 500 m radius and 250 objects; the hard
limits are 5 km and 2,000 objects. This is a diagnostic input to an offline
verification workflow, not a periodic full-map scanner.

The browser map loads the normalized artifact through
`/api/infrastructure-sites/global.geojson`. It displays representative
location markers in the initially hidden `Energy sites` and `Military sites`
layers. This deliberately keeps the strategic overview readable; the SDK
artifact still retains each source geometry. Run
`python examples/sdk/verify_energy_site.py` with a live DCS mission to survey
and temporarily mark one candidate and its nearby scenery objects on the F10
map.

## Site taxonomy

### Energy

- Generation: thermal, nuclear, hydroelectric, biomass, and other historically
  plausible power plants
- Grid: major substations, converter stations, and selected transmission nodes
- Exclude or strongly down-rank modern small solar, wind, biogas, battery, and
  rooftop generation unless the scenario explicitly needs them

Power generation is the best first category: DCS explicitly models it, OSM
usually provides plant boundaries and source tags, and components can be
clustered into understandable sites.

### Fuel and bulk storage

- Refineries and oil terminals
- Tank farms and strategic fuel depots
- Gas storage and major pipeline terminals
- Ammunition or military storage only when supplied by scenario or historical
  evidence

Individual tanks are components, never independent sites by default. Missing
`substance`, `storage`, or parent-site evidence must lower confidence.

### Industry

- Major factories and works
- Steel, chemical, vehicle, machinery, shipbuilding, and other strategically
  relevant production
- Mines, quarries, and processing facilities where DCS represents them

Generic `landuse=industrial` polygons are only search areas. A named works,
factory, product tag, historical source, or confirmed DCS scenery is needed for
a higher-confidence operational site.

### Ports and maritime logistics

- Cargo ports, container terminals, oil terminals, and shipyards
- Naval bases remain a separate military role even when sharing port geometry
- Marinas, leisure harbours, and small fishing facilities are excluded from
  strategic candidates by default

### Rail infrastructure

- Major stations, freight terminals, depots, and marshalling yards
- Rail junctions and rail bridges where network disruption matters
- Ordinary track remains topographic and routing context

The current import contains railway lines but does not yet import enough
station, yard, freight, and depot tags for this model.

### Water, communications, and public utilities

- Dams and major water-control structures
- Major communication or listening sites
- Selected water works only when scenario relevance is explicit
- Wastewater plants are normally geographic context, not strategic targets

Communications and dams need additional OSM tags. The current candidate import
is not sufficient for them.

### Military sites

Military bases, listening posts, naval bases, training areas, and weapons
storage are present in GermanyCW but should not be inferred from civil
infrastructure alone. They can share the common site/component model while
using a separate military-site category and stronger scenario or historical
verification.

## Normalization pipeline

1. Import the category-specific OSM tags required for site discovery.
2. Separate parent sites from component features.
3. Cluster components within parent boundaries and conservative category
   distances.
4. Assign provenance, historical plausibility, confidence, and an explicit DCS
   verification state.
5. Run bounded DCS scenery discovery only around selected candidate sites.
6. Store matched scenery identities and component geometry in the theater
   cache.
7. Expose normalized sites through typed SDK objects and bounded map layers.
8. Calculate category-specific importance and dependencies.
9. Allow scenario policy to create multi-component strategic objectives from
   approved sites.

## DCS verification states

- `unverified`: external source only
- `dcs_scenery_matched`: one or more addressable scenery components found
- `dcs_mission_object_matched`: explicit AIRBASE, STATIC, UNIT, or zone matched
- `dcs_visual_only`: visually represented but no useful addressable component
- `not_represented_in_dcs`: no corresponding DCS representation found
- `historically_uncertain`: modern external site is not reliable for the
  scenario reference year

Verification should also retain DCS map/build version because GermanyCW is
released and updated in phases.

## Recommended implementation order

1. Power plants and major grid sites
2. Refineries, tank farms, and fuel depots
3. Cargo ports, terminals, and shipyards
4. Major factories and industrial works
5. Rail yards, freight terminals, and major stations
6. Dams, mines, tunnels, communications, and military sites

The first implementation should include a reusable bounded DCS scenery survey
so every later category can be checked with the same evidence model.
